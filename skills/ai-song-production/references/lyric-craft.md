# Lyric craft for short AI music videos

Contents: 1 Concept toolkit · 2 Research banks · 3 Lyric rules · 4 Scansion by BPM · 5 Hook · 6 The break · 7 Structures · 8 Checks before locking

## 1. Concept toolkit

- **The knot**: one word or image doing two or three jobs at once. Test it: does it still work if the listener knows only one of the meanings?
  - BLISS = the XP wallpaper + the feeling + the AI "bliss attractor".
  - Siren = the seductive voice that knows everything + the alarm.
  - "To the moon" = getting high + the stock/AI hype chant + the launch. "Don't look down" = vertigo + don't check the chart + denial.
- **Myth or genre frame** that already holds both sides of the argument (Sirens and the mast; a moon rush like a gold rush; Cruella × Pinocchio).
- **One-sentence story** before any lyric: who, what they do, what flips at the end.
- **The deep message fits in one line**, and the hook can carry it literally.
- **Bittersweet beats preachy**: no lecture, no villain. The character knows and chooses anyway.
- **Don't show the symbol literally** if the director resists. Move it into props, set clues and a reveal. A paper-moon set tearing works better than a bubble popping.

## 2. Research banks (for topical songs)

Before writing, research (web search, parallel agents if available):
- **Zeitgeist**: phrases people are actually saying this month. Grade each: A = a casual viewer gets it in one listen, B = most of the target crowd, C = insiders only (graphics only).
- **Pun / knot map**: pairs of meanings between the world of the video and the topic.
- **Devices already used** by the director's previous songs, so they can be banned.
- **Leave-out list**: real private people, likeness and trademark risks, jokes that punch down, lines that could be misheard.

## 3. Lyric rules

1. Every reference lands in one listen. Story first; trivia goes into the graphics.
2. Subtract rather than add: "leave things out rather than force them in".
3. No device from the previous song (countdowns, numbered lists, the same chant shape).
4. At most one proper name per couplet; hard words in spoken lines, not sung ones.
5. Two-reader rule for puns: each works with only one meaning known.
6. Feel beats the metric. A line that scores perfectly but doesn't flow is worse; the director's ear wins.
7. Write the cue for each section as a performance note (see suno-guide §3).
8. Never quote or closely paraphrase existing songs.

## 4. Scansion by BPM

A bar at BPM b lasts 240/b seconds (128 BPM ≈ 1.88 s; Suno will likely play it ~5% faster).
- **Spoken/rapped verse**: one line per 2-bar slot. Measured delivery is ~3.3 syllables/s, so 10–13 syllables fill the slot. 7–9 gets dragged into fragments, and 15+ gets rushed. Put the rhyme on the last stressed word (bar end).
- **Sung lines**: ~2.9 syllables/s; hooks of 4–8 syllables with open vowels on held notes.
- **Chants**: 2–4 syllables, CAPS, with a crowd echo in parentheses.
- Count syllables for every line in a table. Place stressed/important words where the beat falls; don't chop a line into fragments just to hit every beat.
- Muted viewers read the chorus on screen: aim for ~12 characters per second.

## 5. The hook

- A phrase the audience already says (or nearly): "Go touch the grass", "To the moon", "Don't look down".
- 2–7 plain syllables. Title sung/chanted 4+ times, hook 6+ times in a short song.
- Call-and-response gives a K-pop/dance feel: `TO THE MOON! (to the moon)`.
- A hook question that lands in a stop should be syncopated, not square on beats 1–3.

## 6. The break (the sincere line)

- One honest line where the music thins: "I can't touch grass. You can." / "I know it's paper. I'm still here."
- Then the final chorus repeats **unchanged**, and the line re-colours it.
- It's the line most likely to be skipped by Suno (short, quiet section). Check it's sung, and fix with Replace Section if it isn't.

## 7. Structures

**~60–75 s (social / competition)**
`[Intro] whisper 1 line` → `[Verse] 4 spoken lines (or 2 + a contrasting delivery, e.g. intercom)` → `[Pre-Drop] 1 held line` → `[Drop] chant ×2 + tag` → `[Break] sincere line` → `[Build]` → `[Final Drop] chant + changed tag` → `[Stop]` → `[End] whispered hook`.
~100–130 words including echoes.

**~3 min**
Intro · V1 · Pre · Chorus · V2 · Pre · Chorus · Break (sincere line) · Build · Final chorus (unchanged) · Outro. ~240–260 words.

## 8. Checks before locking

- [ ] One-sentence story written; knot works for both readers.
- [ ] Every reference grade A (or moved to graphics).
- [ ] Syllable table done; verse lines 10–13; rhymes on bar ends.
- [ ] Hook ≥6 repeats; title ≥4.
- [ ] Sincere line present in a thinned section.
- [ ] No artist/brand names; no lines from other songs.
- [ ] 2–3 alternates offered for the weakest lines.
- [ ] Director says "locked" → marked in LYRICS.md.
